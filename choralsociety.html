Our Mission

Classical music
belongs to everyone

Founded in 1996 by Conductor Donald Kendrick, the SCSO has spent three decades making large-scale choral-orchestral performance financially accessible to Sacramento-area families. We believe great music doesn't require an exclusive ticket price — it requires community, dedication, and the 180 voices and 55 professional musicians who show up every season.

As the only chorus in the United States with a collective bargaining agreement with a professional orchestra, we keep our players working, our union strong, and our stage open to audiences who might never otherwise experience a live Beethoven symphony.

Veterans Outreach

Since 2012, the SCSO has donated complimentary concert tickets to Veterans and their families as a thank-you for their service to our country and our community.

Children's Music Education

The annual Home for the Holidays concert hosts over 2,300 elementary school children — many experiencing their first live orchestral performance — at no cost to schools.

Accessible Ticket Pricing

The SCSO is committed to keeping tickets affordable for Sacramento families. World-class classical music should not be a luxury available only to the few.

National & International Representation

The SCSO has served as a cultural ambassador for the Sacramento region both nationally and internationally — bringing the community's voice to global stages.

Join the Ensemble

We're always looking for exceptional choral voices

The Sacramento Choral Society welcomes singers of all backgrounds to audition for the 180-voice chorus. Auditions are held seasonally under the direction of Donald Kendrick, Music Director and Conductor.

To inquire about auditioning, contact our office or email us with your voice part and experience. We'll be in touch with audition dates and requirements for the current season.

Season 30 · The Legacy

Three decades.
One conductor.
One community.

When Donald Kendrick founded the Sacramento Choral Society & Orchestra in 1996, he built something Sacramento didn't know it needed: a professional-grade choral organization with a union orchestra, accessible pricing, and an uncompromising commitment to the music. Thirty seasons later, the ensemble has performed over 180 concerts, produced 10 mastered recordings, and raised nearly three million dollars to keep professional musicians working in this region.
